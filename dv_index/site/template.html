California DV Law Index

Laws that are about domestic violence, and laws that never say so but still change what survivors can get: housing, immigration status, custody, firearms, benefits, courts and privacy.

California bills Federal law Codebook

Flagged means the tool picked a bill out as likely to matter to DV survivors. Direct bills name domestic, dating or family violence, or change a DV law. Indirect bills don't say "domestic violence" but change something survivors rely on, such as housing, immigration status, custody, guns or benefits, and use victim or safety language.

These labels are predictions from a keyword-and-theme tool, not yet checked against hand coding. Read the bill before relying on a label.

Who a law reaches

Pick one identity, or several to see laws that reach all of them at once. A bill counts only when its own text uses that group's language, so some groups are small. The number on each button is how many bills you would see if you added it.

No 2025–26 bill names a religion, an ethnicity or a South Asian language, so bills can't be sorted by community. For Muslim and South Asian survivors, advocates most often raise immigration and VAWA, language access, faith, and forced marriage (under Woman). None of these is unique to those communities.

How bills are flagged

Source

The Legislature's official bulk data (downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov). For each AB and SB bill, the tool reads the latest version's title, subject and Legislative Counsel's Digest.

Direct

The bill names domestic, intimate partner, dating or family violence, or cites a section of the Domestic Violence Prevention Act (Family Code §§6200–6409).

Indirect

The bill touches an impact area (housing, immigration, custody, firearms and so on) and uses victim or safety language. Some citations count on their own, such as Civil Code §1946.7, which lets victims end a lease.

The federal layer

Federal law sets a floor that California builds on. These are the federal statutes and regulations WomensLaw selects for survivors. They aren't in the index of California bills, but many state bills only make sense next to them. Not all are about DV: each is marked yes if it is about DV, partly if it is a general law with a DV provision, or no if it is listed for context.

Source: WomensLaw.org (National Network to End Domestic Violence), Selected Federal Statutes, updated December 17, 2025, and its federal pages on VAWA housing protections, federal gun laws and domestic violence in the military.
